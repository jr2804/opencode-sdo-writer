# ETSI IPR Regime

The ETSI IPR Policy is **Annex 6 of the Rules of Procedure** and is
normative. The *Guide on IPR* explains it and yields to the Policy: where
they conflict, the Policy prevails. The full text is in `etsi-directives`
(Chapter 4) and the summary in its Core Frameworks; this file collects what
matters when drafting or participating.

## The regime in one table

| Aspect | ETSI rule |
|--------|-----------|
| Instrument | Rules of Procedure Annex 6 (IPR Policy), normative; Guide on IPR, explanatory |
| Disclosure duty | Reasonable endeavours to inform ETSI **where you participate** -- explicitly **no duty to search** |
| Scope of a disclosure | One member of a patent family covers the family |
| Essentiality | A **technical** test: impossible to implement without infringing |
| Commitment | The **Director-General immediately requests a FRAND undertaking**; it covers the family and binds successors-in-interest (6.1bis); it may be conditioned on reciprocity |
| Chair duties | Normative (Annex C): proper **scope statements**; a **Call for IPRs at the start of every meeting** plus milestone reminders; record the call and the responses; report essential IPRs to Legal **immediately** |
| Licensing | Licensing rates and terms are **never discussed in Technical Bodies**; the only lawful channel is voluntary, unilateral, public **ex ante** disclosure |

## Antitrust interaction

A standards meeting is also a meeting of competitors. Discussing licensing
rates or terms in a Technical Body is a competition-law problem, not a
technical one -- the Antitrust Guidelines are explicit that the law controls
where the Guidelines and the law conflict. See `etsi-directives` (Ch 5).

## Drafting rules

- The IPR clause in an ETSI deliverable is a **protected clause**: keep it
  **unmodified**. The skeleton in `sdo-drafting` (`assets/etsi/`) carries the
  correct text.
- Deliverables are **English only** and use the **mandatory editHelp!
  skeletons**; the IPR and modal-verb clauses must not be touched.
- A contribution that proposes text possibly requiring a patent should say so
  at the meeting, so the Chair can make the Call for IPRs and record the
  response.
