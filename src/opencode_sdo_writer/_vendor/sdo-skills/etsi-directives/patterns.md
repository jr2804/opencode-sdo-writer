# Patterns

## Locate the Governing Instrument

**When to use**: any question of the form "what are the rules on X?"
**How**: identify the subject → name the instrument (Statutes, RoP, TWP, Financial Regulations, a Code of Conduct, a ToR, the IPR Policy/Guide, the Antitrust Guidelines, the EDR, the Information Policy) → apply that instrument's own numbering. Never cite "the Directives" alone.
**Trade-offs**: naming the instrument is slower than quoting a summary, but it is the only way to know the amendment threshold and the decision-maker.

## Determine the Correct Voting Procedure

**When to use**: before any decision, or when drafting a proposal.
**How**: ask what is being decided → if it is dissolution, an RoP dispute, an EN, a Statutes/RoP amendment or a new National Delegation weighting, use **Weighted National Voting** (11.2.1); if it is Full-member rights, an EU-regulatory document, an EU standardization policy or an EU-only Work Programme priority, use **Weighted Individual Voting by Full members** (11.2.2); **otherwise it is 11.2.3** (Full + Associate, weighted individually). Then apply the quorum and majority rules for that procedure.
**Trade-offs**: 11.2.3 is the default — but only after you have positively excluded the two enumerated lists, not by assumption.

## Amend a Governance Instrument

**When to use**: any change proposal to the Directives.
**How**: Statutes or RoP → Specially Convened GA, ≥ 6 weeks' notice with agenda and proposed text, **75 %** (Statutes) or **71 %** (RoP incl. annexes) of total weighted votes. Other instruments → their adopting body (GA, Board, or Director-General); the starred Board instruments (TWP, IPR Guide, Antitrust Guidelines) may also be agreed at a GA meeting.
**Trade-offs**: the SCM route is slow but the only valid one for the two constitutional instruments; a defective amendment is void regardless of support.

## Run the Membership Approval Process (ES / EG)

**When to use**: adopting or non-editorially revising an ETSI Standard or ETSI Guide.
**How**: TB approves the draft → submit to the Secretariat **within 14 days** → Secretariat prepares for members **within 30 days** if EDR-compliant → **60-day** all-member vote (YES / NO / abstention, NO requires comments; abstentions excluded) → success: publish **within 10 days, editorial changes only**; failure: TB Chair decides and receives the comments.
**Trade-offs**: broad legitimacy and a long clock; use TS/TR when a membership vote is disproportionate.

## Run ENAP or SRdAP (European Standards)

**When to use**: a European Standard, from ETSI's own proposal or from an EC Standardisation Request.
**How**: ETSI-originated → **ENAP**: ≥ 4 members propose, Standstill (from +60 days) and Public Enquiry run, then **NSOG** votes by Weighted National Voting. SReq-derived → **SRdAP**: NSBG holds exclusive competence for acceptance, WISR approval and deliverable adoption/withdrawal; the GoSR launches the **25-day** NSBG vote and the DG notifies the EC/EFTA **within 30 days**.
**Trade-offs**: two lanes with different voters and clocks — mixing them invalidates the process.

## Create and Manage a Work Item

**When to use**: starting or steering any standardization task.
**How**: pick the route (**WI** proposed by members vs **WISR** from an SReq); secure **≥ 4 Full/Associate supporters**; record all parameters (title, deliverable type, scope, schedule, supporters, Rapporteur, plus user/SME/security/privacy/environmental/labour aspects); name **one Rapporteur**; the TG **adopts** the WI; track **decision** and **maturity** milestones; use change control if agreed; **stop** it when no longer required — and it **shall** be stopped after **one year** without progress.
**Trade-offs**: the parameters that slow creation are the ones that feed later compliance (SME/user/security aspects, annex scope).

## Appoint or Replace a Technical Group Chair

**When to use**: a Chair vacancy, resignation, incapacity or loss of support.
**How**: Chair must be a delegate of a Full/Associate member **and** a member of the group; call for candidature; one candidate → appoint by Consensus, several → Clause 1.7 vote (secret ballot where required); **Board confirms**. On loss of supporting member: **three-month transition**, Vice-Chair as interim, former Chair attends as Guest; if unresolved, the group assumes resignation and the period still counts as a full term.
**Trade-offs**: term limits bind both the person and the member organization or Group.

## Open a Meeting Compliantly

**When to use**: chairing any Technical Body or working group meeting.
**How**: deliver the three mandatory reminders — **Antitrust** (including the forbidden-topic list), the **Call for IPRs** (with the declaration route via ipr.etsi.org), and the **Code of Conduct highlights** — then keep to the Code's conduct rules (speakers recognized, no interruption, short interventions, non-native speakers' views considered, conflicts disclosed).
**Trade-offs**: it costs minutes and is itself a normative duty; omitting it is a breach in its own right.

## Handle an IPR Disclosure End to End

**When to use**: an Essential or potentially Essential IPR comes to light.
**How**: check the WI scope statement is adequate → disclose by reasonable endeavours (no search duty; one family member covers the family) → use the declaration form via ipr.etsi.org → Chair informs the Legal Department immediately and records the call and response → DG requests the FRAND undertaking → if refused: viable-alternative review, then (pre-publication) cessation of work with the member/third-party escalation paths and the **3-month** answer window, or (post-publication) the Clause 8.2 ladder ending in a request to the EC including possible non-recognition.
**Trade-offs**: the regime is designed to surface blockers early; late disclosure can be sanctioned as a Policy breach.

## Decide an STF Funding Question

**When to use**: Board decisions on Specialist Task Force funding.
**How**: require an authorized presenter (Reference Body Chair/Vice-Chair); every Board member with any personal or professional, direct or indirect financial interest **discloses**; disclosed members and the presenter may observe but not intervene and **must leave during decision-making**; decide within the GA's financial framework, or only propose to the GA for STFs outside it.
**Trade-offs**: the strictest conflict rules in the Directives — non-compliance is itself treated as a conflict.

## Route an Appeal

**When to use**: contesting a decision.
**How**: against a Technical Organization decision → **Board** (RoP 22.2) or the **GA**; against a Board decision → **GA**; against a Chair's ruling in a Technical Group → **Board**, but the ruling **stands as the basis for future operations unless overturned**. Also: member-vs-Institute claims must exhaust internal appeals before legal proceedings.
**Trade-offs**: appeals are procedural, not suspensive — plan around the ruling remaining in force.

## Admit or Expel a Member

**When to use**: membership status changes.
**How**: admission → Consensus via the **four-times-yearly online poll**, escalating to the next Ordinary GA if not reached; dues scale 100/75/50/25 % by poll number. Non-payment → expulsion by the **DG** (with suspension of voting and participation from 1 June); other substantial breach → **GA** decision with registered-letter notice and an opportunity to be heard.
**Trade-offs**: the decision-maker follows the character of the case; the procedural safeguard attaches only to the GA route.

## Draft a Compliant Provision

**When to use**: writing any normative text in a deliverable.
**How**: choose the modal verb that matches the intent — **shall** (requirement), **should** (recommendation), **may** (permission), **can** (possibility), **will** (out-of-scope behaviour), **is** (fact); use the mandatory skeleton; keep the IPR and modal-verbs clauses unmodified; keep requirements out of the Foreword.
**Trade-offs**: the discipline resists casual drafting, which is precisely its point — the verb is the legal effect.

## Clear Third-Party Material

**When to use**: wanting to include text, a figure, a trademark, software or a photograph in a deliverable.
**How**: **reference rather than reproduce**; use the correct generic term instead of a trade name; if reproduction is unavoidable, obtain the owner's authorisation, mark the nature with **©**, always reference source and owner, and inform the **Secretariat early**. For software, avoid reproduction; for open source, **assess contamination risk before inclusion**.
**Trade-offs**: early Secretariat involvement lengthens the drafting loop but prevents an unpublishable deliverable.

## Create an ISG or SDG

**When to use**: the standard Technical Organization routes do not fit.
**How**: ISG → ≥ 4 members (or applicants) request the DG; purpose and expected impact on the TO Work Programme defined up front; GR/GS only; **no EC/EFTA-mandated work**. SDG → ≥ 4 members (or applicants) request the DG; **SDG Member Agreement with CLA** (plus optional Participant/Individual Contributor agreements); ToR define contribution and publication licences; own budget; **no ETSI Deliverables**.
**Trade-offs**: speed and flexibility, at the cost of a narrower deliverable palette and explicit exclusions.

## Release Technical Documents Publicly

**When to use**: making TC/EP/ISG material visible outside the membership.
**How**: apply **TWP Clause 6** — separate conditions for draft deliverables, individual contributions, and any other documents from open Docbox areas; check the definitions in RoP Annex 1 and TWP Annex A; treat the Information Policy as orientation only.
**Trade-offs**: openness supports the Values and antitrust transparency, but the conditions are document-type specific.
