# Chapter 12: ETSI Information Policy

**Version adopted by the Director-General (5 December 2025).** A short, **non-normative** instrument; it carries an informative Annex 1 on the ETSI structure.

## Core Idea

The Information Policy is a **simplified summary** of access rules that are actually set elsewhere in the Directives — which means it is a convenience layer, not a source of new obligations, and it **yields to the Directives on any discrepancy**.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Status: non-normative by its own terms.** The Policy "provides a simplified summary of the rules set out in various parts of the ETSI Directives", and as a non-normative document **"it does not replace or override the ETSI Directives, which shall prevail in case of discrepancy"**.
  - When to use: orienting yourself or a newcomer on what is visible to whom — but cite the Directives, not this Policy, when the rule matters.
- **Scope of application**: the Policy applies to the **ETSI website** (<https://www.etsi.org>), the **ETSI Portal** (portal.etsi.org), the **ETSI document repository** (docbox.etsi.org), and all ETSI-related sites.
  - When to use: determining whether an access question is even within the Policy's reach.
- **Subject matter**: it describes **the access rights of different categories of ETSI members and non-members** — i.e. who may see which materials, across those platforms.
  - When to use: the first place to look for a quick answer on document visibility, before tracing the underlying provision.
- **Annex 1 (informative): ETSI Structure**: a simplified overview of the ETSI bodies.
  - When to use: orientation on how the organs fit together (compare Ch 2's Statutes Art. 10 and Ch 3's RoP Art. 2, which are the normative sources).
- **Definitional pointer**: the Policy directs the reader to **Annex 1 of the Rules of Procedure** and **Annex A of the Technical Working Procedures** for definitions — not to itself.
  - When to use: this is the tell that the Policy is a navigational aid; the definitional authorities are the RoP and TWP annexes.

## Key Concepts

- **Non-normative instrument**: explanatory only; no independent binding force.
- **Prevailing instrument**: the Directives, on any discrepancy.
- **Covered surfaces**: ETSI website, Portal, Docbox repository, and all ETSI-related sites.
- **Access categories**: members (by status) and non-members.
- **Definitional authorities**: RoP Annex 1 and TWP Annex A.

## Mental Models

- "**A map, not the territory**" — the Policy tells you where the access rules are and roughly what they say; the Directives hold the rules.
- "**Prevail-clause first**" — whenever this instrument and another disagree, the other one wins; check before relying on it.
- "**Four surfaces**" — website, Portal, Docbox, and related sites are the Policy's whole jurisdiction.
- "**Definitions live next door**" — the Policy deliberately points to the RoP and TWP annexes rather than defining terms itself.

## Anti-patterns

- **Citing the Information Policy as the authority for an access rule**: it is non-normative and expressly yields to the Directives — cite the underlying instrument (e.g. TWP Clause 6 on making working documents public, or the RoP provisions on document access).
- **Assuming the Policy creates rights**: it summarises rights created elsewhere; it does not create them.
- **Reading it as complete**: "simplified summary" means deliberately abbreviated — the underlying provisions may be more detailed or conditional.
- **Ignoring the definitional cross-references**: the substantive definitions are in RoP Annex 1 and TWP Annex A.

## Worked Example

### "Who can see a Technical Body's draft and contributions?"

1. **Do not answer from the Information Policy alone** — it summarises, and the Directives prevail.
2. **Go to the operative provision**: **TWP Clause 6** sets the conditions for making TC/EP/ISG **draft deliverables**, **individual contributions**, and **any other documents** public from open Docbox areas (Ch 11).
3. **Check the definitions**: RoP Annex 1 (member categories, statuses) and TWP Annex A determine what "member", "contribution" and the relevant categories mean.
4. **Use the Information Policy as orientation only** — for a first-pass answer or a pointer, not as the citation.

- Takeaway: treat the Policy the way its own text instructs — as a summary whose authority is borrowed from the instruments it summarises.

## Key Takeaways

1. The Information Policy is non-normative and expressly subordinate to the Directives on discrepancy.
2. It summarises the access rights of member categories and non-members across the ETSI website, Portal and Docbox repository.
3. Its informative Annex 1 gives a simplified ETSI structure overview.
4. Its definitions point to RoP Annex 1 and TWP Annex A.
5. For any access question that matters, cite TWP Clause 6 or the relevant RoP provision, not this Policy.
