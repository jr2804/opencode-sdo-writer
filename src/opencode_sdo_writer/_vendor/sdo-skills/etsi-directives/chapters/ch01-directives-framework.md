# Chapter 1: The ETSI Directives Framework

## Core Idea

The ETSI Directives are not a single document — they are a **compilation of 17 separate governance instruments** (version 53, 1 September 2026), each adopted by a different body, each with its own amendment threshold. Knowing *which instrument governs a question* and *who can change it* is the first and most frequently needed skill.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The Directives as a compilation**: the published "ETSI Directives" (portal.etsi.org/resources/ETSIdirectives.aspx) bundles the individual documents into one PDF with a shared version number and a shared history table.
  - When to use: any question of the form "where is the rule on X?" — the answer is a named instrument, not "the Directives".
  - How: locate the instrument by subject, then apply its own article/clause numbering (numbering restarts in every instrument).
- **Adoption hierarchy (who adopts what)**: the adoption body determines how much notice and what majority is needed to change a rule.
  - **Heads of National Delegations**: Statutes; Rules of Procedure.
  - **General Assembly**: ETSI Values; Code of Conduct for ETSI members; Code of Conduct for Board members; Powers and Functions delegated to the Board; Financial Regulations; ToR of the Finance Committee (FC); ToR of the Group on Standardisation Requests (GoSR).
  - **Board**: Board Working Procedures; ToR of the Operational Coordination Group (OCG); Technical Working Procedures*; Guide on Intellectual Property Rights*; Guidelines for Antitrust Compliance*; Rights and obligations for ETSI courtesy title holders, ETSI Fellows and Retired Friends of ETSI.
  - **Director-General**: Information Policy; ETSI Drafting Rules.
  - *Changes to the Technical Working Procedures, the Guide on IPR, or the Guidelines for Antitrust Compliance may be agreed during a meeting of the General Assembly.*
  - When to use: planning an amendment, or judging how much weight an instrument carries.
- **The amendment ladder**: two tiers, defined by the Statutes.
  - **Statutes and Rules of Procedure**: amendable only by the General Assembly in a **Specially Convened Meeting** called with **not less than six (6) weeks' notice**, the notice accompanied by an agenda (Statutes Art. 18).
  - **Everything else**: changed by its adopting body under its own procedures; the three starred Board instruments may also be agreed at a GA meeting.
  - When to use: any change proposal — establishing the correct procedure first prevents a defective amendment.
- **Precedence rule**: where a Guide explains a Policy, the **Policy takes precedence in all cases**. The Guide on IPR states this explicitly about the IPR Policy; the same explanatory-vs-normative distinction applies to the Directives' other guides.
  - When to use: resolving a conflict between an explanatory guide and its normative parent document.
- **Version history as a change-log**: the Directives carry a history table from **Version 1 (March 1988)** — the first edition comprised only the Statutes and Rules of Procedure — to the current version, recording each change with its authorizing decision reference (e.g. `[GA(25)85a_008r1]`, `[BOARD(23)144a_012]`).
  - When to use: determining when a rule changed, or finding the decision that authorized it.
  - Structural eras recorded: 1988–1997 (original structure: General Assembly, Technical Assembly, TCC); V5 March 1993 added the ETSI IPR Policy and Undertaking; V7 November 1994 added the interim IPR Policy as Annex 6; V10 July 1996 added Annex 7 (Rules of Operation for the Board) and Board Powers and Functions.

## Key Concepts

- **Instrument**: one of the 17 separately adopted documents inside the compilation.
- **Adopting body**: the organ competent to amend a given instrument (Heads of National Delegations, General Assembly, Board, or Director-General).
- **Specially Convened Meeting**: a GA meeting called specifically to amend the Statutes and/or Rules of Procedure; requires ≥ 6 weeks' notice.
- **Counsellor**: EC and EFTA-secretariat representatives with special status and **no right to vote** (Statutes Art. 11.3).
- **Decision reference**: the bracket tag identifying the originating decision (e.g. `[BOARD(24)149_027r1]`).
- **Normative vs informative annex**: annexes marked "(NORMATIVE)" carry requirements; "(INFORMATIVE)" annexes explain (e.g. TWP Annexes P–U are largely informative).
- **Void annexes**: several annexes are explicitly "VOID" (e.g. RoP Annexes 4, 5; TWP Annexes F, I, O; Guide on IPR Annex A/B) — retained as placeholders so numbering stays stable.

## Mental Models

- "Think of the Directives as a **statute book, not a book**" — you look up an instrument, not a page.
- "Ask who adopts it before asking what it says" — the adopting body tells you the change threshold and the political weight.
- "Guides explain, Policies bind" — when they disagree, the normative instrument wins.
- "Every number restarts" — "Article 4" exists in the Statutes, the Rules of Procedure, and the Financial Regulations with three different meanings; always qualify the citation.
- "Void annexes are load-bearing" — numbering stability is deliberate, not sloppiness.

## Anti-patterns

- **Citing "the Directives" without the instrument**: ambiguous — always name the instrument and its clause (e.g. "RoP Art. 13.2.3", not "Directives 13.2.3").
- **Trusting a Guide over its Policy**: the Guide on IPR explicitly defers to the IPR Policy; a Guide cannot override the normative text.
- **Assuming a normal GA majority can amend the Statutes or RoP**: the Specially Convened Meeting with six weeks' notice is mandatory — a defective amendment is void.
- **Treating a VOID annex as deleted content**: void means retired-in-place; numbering after it is unaffected.
- **Missing the starred-instrument exception**: TWP, the IPR Guide, and the Antitrust Guidelines (Board-adopted) may also be agreed at a GA meeting — an easy procedural trap.

## Worked Example

**"A Board member wants the Rules of Procedure changed."** Trace the route:

1. Identify the instrument: the Rules of Procedure (not the Board Working Procedures, which governs the Board's own operation).
2. Identify the adopting body: Heads of National Delegations.
3. Apply the threshold: Statutes Art. 18 — only a Specially Convened Meeting of the General Assembly, with **not less than six weeks' notice** and an accompanying agenda.
4. Consequence: a change voted at an Ordinary Meeting — even unanimously — would not be a valid amendment, regardless of the merits.
5. Contrast: the same member wanting to change the **Board Working Procedures** needs only Board adoption (or, where the instrument is a starred one, agreement at a GA meeting).

- Takeaway: the procedural route is determined before the substantive debate — check the ladder first.

## Key Takeaways

1. The Directives are 17 instruments in one compilation; always name the instrument.
2. Statutes and Rules of Procedure require a Specially Convened GA with ≥ 6 weeks' notice; other instruments follow their adopting body.
3. TWP, the IPR Guide, and the Antitrust Guidelines may also be agreed at a GA meeting — the starred exception.
4. Guides explain; Policies and normative instruments bind — the Policy prevails on conflict.
5. Clause numbering restarts per instrument, and VOID annexes are deliberate placeholders.
