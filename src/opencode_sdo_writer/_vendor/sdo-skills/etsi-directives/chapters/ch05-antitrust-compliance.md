# Chapter 5: Guidelines for Antitrust Compliance

**Version adopted by Board #133 (10 June 2021).** Three parts: **B** background on EU competition law, **C** the compliance Guidelines proper, **D** a "do's and don'ts" mnemonic for TC/WG participants.

## Core Idea

ETSI's status as a recognized European standards body **does not exempt it, its members or its activities from competition law** — because ETSI is simultaneously an *association of undertakings* under Article 101 TFEU. Standardization is a legitimate, EU-endorsed pooling of resources, but the meetings where it happens are also where competitors sit in one room, so the Guidelines forbid a specific list of topics and avoid even the *appearance* of restraint.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The dual character of ETSI (B.2.2)**: ETSI encourages members to pool resources to produce standards — an objective endorsed by the EU — while being at the same time an **association of undertakings** within Article 101(1) TFEU. Members must not abuse their attendance at ETSI meetings to establish or further restrictive agreements (e.g. price fixing).
  - When to use: explaining why standardization activity is lawful but meeting conduct is tightly constrained.
- **Article 101 TFEU — anti-competitive agreements (B.2)**: prohibits agreements between undertakings, decisions of associations of undertakings, and concerted practices having the object or effect of restricting competition within the EU. Applies to both **horizontal** (actual/potential competitors at the same level) and **vertical** agreements.
  - **Five prohibited categories**: (a) directly or indirectly fixing purchase/selling prices or other trading conditions; (b) limiting or controlling production, markets, technical development or investment; (c) sharing markets or sources of supply; (d) applying dissimilar conditions to equivalent transactions, placing a party at a competitive disadvantage; (e) making contracts subject to supplementary obligations unconnected by nature or commercial usage.
  - **Consequence**: prohibited agreements are **automatically void**.
  - **Form is irrelevant** (B.2.4): even unwritten agreements, gentlemen's agreements and other tacit understandings count.
- **The horizontal risk list for ETSI (B.2.5)** — the practices that "might in principle arise in the context of ETSI's activities":
  - **Price fixing** — one of the most serious infringements; **technology pools where the pooled technologies compete with each other** may raise price-fixing concerns.
  - **Market and customer allocation** — allocating markets by territory, product or customer.
  - **Restrictions in licences of intellectual property rights** — bi- or multilateral IPR exchanges may in specific cases fall under 101(1).
  - **Boycotts** — preventing new entrants or excluding an existing player; an **unjustified refusal to admit a new party** can be assimilated to a boycott, which is why **admission rules must rest on clear, neutral and objective criteria**. Generally, no ETSI activity shall encourage anyone to refrain from purchasing from any supplier, or from dealing with any supplier.
  - **Vertical** practices (e.g. resale price maintenance) are equally covered.
- **Limited exceptions (B.2.6)**: Article 101(3) can declare 101(1) inapplicable where an agreement improves production or distribution or promotes technical/economic progress while allowing consumers a fair share of the benefit, imposing no restrictions indispensable to those objectives and not affording the elimination of competition in a substantial part of the products concerned. The Commission has also adopted **block exemption regulations** (e.g. for technology transfer) and published guidelines on their assessment.
- **Article 102 TFEU — abuse of dominance (B.3)**: prohibits abuse by one or more undertakings of a dominant position within the internal market or a substantial part of it, insofar as it may affect trade between Member States.
  - **Four cumulative criteria (B.3.2)**: dominant position (capacity to behave independently of competitors and customers); abuse of that position; negative effect on trade between Member States; and no objective justification.
  - **Critical warning**: the fact that ETSI itself is not dominant does **not** mean its members can never abuse a dominant position in ETSI Committees — caution is required. Abuse is a **question of fact and degree**, with "normal industry practices" as the reference.
  - **Prohibited practices (B.3.3)**: unfairly high or predatory low pricing; **fidelity rebates**; **abuse of IPRs** — the mere existence of a patent, trademark or copyright is not enough for dominance, but a dominant IPR holder's refusal to license is abusive where (i) the licence is **indispensable** to enter a market, (ii) the refusal **eliminates all competition** in the market for the products/services concerned, (iii) it **prevents the offer of new products or services for which there is potential consumer demand**, and (iv) it is **not objectively justified**; **tying clauses**; and other discriminatory or unfair conditions imposed on users or contractual partners.
- **Enforcement and consequences (B.4)**: the **European Commission** has investigative powers and its decisions can be appealed to the European Court of Justice; **Member State competition authorities** apply EU and national rules alongside it; **EFTA States** are supervised by the EFTA Surveillance Authority. Infringements are commonly brought to national courts/authorities; an offending agreement is **unenforceable**; and significant fines may be imposed.
- **The compliance Guidelines (C)** — "shall be observed" by all participants in standardization activities:
  - **Membership (C.1)**: admission based on **clear, neutral and objective criteria**, open to all interested parties on reasonable terms and conditions without unfair restrictions; **every refusal must be justified**, and the excluded person/company must **always be given an opportunity for explanation**.
  - **Participation in technical work (C.2)**: participation should be **open**, so that all members potentially affected by a proposal can participate; rules and procedures must be **transparent** — sufficiently specified, clear and detailed — so participants can understand the procedure in force; **in case of doubt, ask for clarification**.
  - **Meetings (C.3)**: the Chair and/or Secretary prepares each meeting per **Technical Working Procedures §1.5**, with particular attention to transparency; **concise minutes** are kept and meeting reports prepared per **TWP §1.9.3**.
  - **Activities in ETSI (C.4.1)** — the core prohibition. Discussions, communications or any exchange of information **in or on the edge of any ETSI meeting** (explicitly: informal discussions, social gatherings, corridor talks) must **not** cover:
    - pricing strategies or product pricing;
    - terms and conditions of sale including discounts, allowances, credit terms;
    - production levels or capacity;
    - limitation of technical development or investment;
    - allocation of sales territories, markets or customers;
    - market shares;
    - submitted bids or intentions to bid;
    - preventing anybody from gaining access to any market or customer for goods and services;
    - refusals to deal or do business with competitors, vendors or suppliers;
    - ongoing or threatened litigation.
    - **"Even the appearance" counts**: any discussion that *appears* to be leading to restraints on competition must be carefully avoided.
  - **The ex ante disclosure carve-out (C.4.2)**: **voluntary, unilateral, public, ex ante disclosure of licensing terms** by licensors of essential IPRs is permitted, for the sole purpose of assisting members in making informed **unilateral and independent** decisions. ETSI takes no position and is not responsible for determining whether the disclosed terms are FRAND.
  - **Duty to report (C.4.3)**: a participant who becomes aware of any discussion or exchange that appears to lead to restraints on competition **must** report it.
  - **Other (C.5)**: early knowledge of Essential IPRs is required (IPR Policy Clause 4.1 compliance supports this); any **conditions pertaining to the use** of documents produced from an ETSI Work Item must be **visible in the ETSI documentation**; **nobody should be coerced** into adopting any ETSI document; and — the controlling clause — **in any inconsistency between the Guidelines and the Antitrust Laws, the Antitrust Laws control**.
- **"Do's and don'ts" (D)** — the mnemonic:
  - **Do**: use best reasonable efforts to comply with competition laws in all ETSI activities; comply with the rules when chairing and when attending; **focus discussions on standardization issues only**; remember standards should promote competition and benefit consumers; build standards on solutions best meeting the technical objectives; comply with IPR Guide §4.1 and Antitrust §4.2 if disclosing price and terms; consult the ETSI Legal Advisor and/or your own counsel on concern.
  - **Do not**: engage in activities intended to restrain competition or harm consumers; attempt to set or control price or terms of product, service or licence fees; discuss licensing price/terms, product or service price/terms, pricing methods, profits, margins, cost data, production plans, market share or territories; **attend meetings where procedural rules are not followed**.

## Key Concepts

- **Association of undertakings**: ETSI's competition-law character alongside its standards-body role — the reason the Guidelines exist.
- **Horizontal / vertical agreements**: competitor-to-competitor / different-level agreements; Article 101 covers both.
- **Automatic voidness**: the sanction for a prohibited agreement.
- **Abuse of dominance**: conduct (not mere possession of a dominant position) that is unjustified and harms cross-border trade.
- **Fidelity rebate**: a rebate conditional on buying all or most requirements from the dominant supplier — abusive.
- **Ex ante disclosure carve-out**: permitted voluntary unilateral licensing-term disclosure, with ETSI taking no FRAND position.
- **Antitrust Laws control**: the Guidelines yield to the law on inconsistency.

## Mental Models

- "**A standards meeting is also a competitors' meeting**" — lawful collaboration on technology, unlawful collaboration on markets.
- "**Topic discipline, not intent**" — the prohibition is on topics; whether the exchange was aimed at a restraint is beside the point.
- "**Even the appearance is a violation**" — the corridor and the coffee break are inside the perimeter.
- "**Openness is the antitrust control**" — open participation, transparent procedures, justified admission refusals, recorded minutes; each is an antitrust measure, not just good manners.
- "**The Guidelines are a floor, the law is the ceiling**" — where they diverge, the law controls.

## Anti-patterns

- **Assuming the ESO privilege confers antitrust immunity**: expressly denied in the Introduction and B.2.2.
- **Discussing licensing terms or prices in a Technical Body**: on the forbidden list — with the narrow C.4.2 ex ante carve-out as the only permitted route, and that outside the TB's technical discussion.
- **Treating informal settings as out of scope**: social gatherings and corridor talks are named explicitly.
- **Refusing admission without justification or explanation**: an unjustified refusal can be assimilated to a boycott; every refusal must be justified and the excluded party heard.
- **Letting a meeting proceed without following the procedures**: participants are told not to attend meetings where procedural rules are not followed.
- **Recording nothing**: concise minutes per TWP §1.9.3 are a compliance control, and C.4.3 imposes a duty to report observed restraints.
- **Believing ETSI is responsible for judging FRAND terms it receives ex ante**: ETSI expressly takes no position.

## Worked Example

**A WG dinner after a plenary.** Three delegates from competing manufacturers discuss "how expensive it has become to implement the new profile, and where we'd each price our chips."

1. **Setting**: a social gathering on the edge of an ETSI meeting — expressly within C.4.1's scope.
2. **Topics**: "where we'd each price our chips" is *product pricing*; comparing implementation costs edges toward *cost data*. Both are on the forbidden list.
3. **Appearance**: even if nothing is agreed, the exchange *appears* to be leading to a restraint — C.4.1 requires that appearance to be avoided, not merely a final agreement.
4. **Escalation**: any participant who becomes aware of such an exchange **must** report it (C.4.3).
5. **The lawful alternative**: if a participant wants to make licensing terms known, the permitted route is a **voluntary, unilateral, public, ex ante disclosure** (C.4.2 / IPR Guide §4.1) — a published statement, not a bilateral dinner negotiation.

- Takeaway: the perimeter is drawn around *topics*, not venues or intentions — and the lawful channel for licensing information is publication, not conversation.

## Key Takeaways

1. ETSI is a standards body *and* an association of undertakings — competition law applies to it and its members without exemption.
2. Article 101 targets horizontal and vertical restraints; price fixing, market allocation, IPR-licence restrictions and boycotts are the ETSI-context risks.
3. Article 102 adds abuse of dominance, including refusal to license where the licence is indispensable, all competition is eliminated, new products are prevented, and there is no objective justification.
4. C.4.1 bans a defined topic list in and around meetings, and bans even the *appearance* of restraint; C.4.3 imposes a duty to report.
5. Openness, transparency, justified admission decisions and recorded minutes are antitrust controls, not administrative formalities.
6. The only permitted licensing-terms channel is voluntary, unilateral, public ex ante disclosure — and ETSI takes no position on its FRAND-ness.
