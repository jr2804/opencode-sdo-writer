# Chapter 10: Courtesy Titles, ETSI Fellows and Retired Friends of ETSI

**Version adopted by Board #153 (17–19 June 2025).** A short instrument recognising long service — and, more importantly for practice, defining precisely **what these honours do not permit**.

## Core Idea

ETSI recognises three kinds of distinction, each with a different gatekeeper: **courtesy titles** granted by the General Assembly by consensus, **ETSI Fellowships** decided by an Award Committee on individual merit, and **Retired Friends of ETSI** status approved administratively by the Director-General. None of them confers any authority to speak for ETSI, and the instrument ends by restricting what honorees may do with ETSI documentation.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **ETSI courtesy title (§1)**:
  - **Eligibility**: any former ETSI Chair or Vice-Chair; any former Board member; any former member of the Secretariat.
  - **Nomination**: the **General Assembly** nominates and grants courtesy titles, **based on consensus**, and decides on the title itself.
  - **Criteria**: the title is **only** meant to recognise **outstanding contribution to ETSI over time**, given to an individual whose dedication to ETSI is well known and who has fulfilled an official role; the GA proposes and grants it.
- **ETSI Fellowship (§2)** — the substantively richest award:
  - **Eligibility**: any current or former participant in ETSI work (representative of a current or former ETSI member); any current or former participant in an **ETSI Partnership Project** (not necessarily an ETSI member representative); any current or former representative of an **ETSI Partner or Counsellor**; any former member of the Secretariat. **Nominees need not have held an official position**, and a Fellowship may be awarded **posthumously**.
  - **Nomination**: any individual representative of an ETSI member — **except members of the Award Committee** — may propose **a maximum of one candidate per call**. To be eligible, a candidate must be nominated by **at least four (4) individuals not belonging to the same ETSI member or Academic/Corporate/Public Group as the nominee**, and not to one another's group. Nominations from a **varied mix of member categories are strongly recommended**, and nominations **remain confidential**. Candidates need not be consulted before nomination, but **their permission is required before publishing any details** if awarded.
  - **Criteria**: recognises outstanding contribution to ETSI over time; **there is no automatic granting** based on position or tenure. Recipients should be recognised for one of: an **outstanding personal contribution** — a clear personal contribution to a major innovation or transformation in ETSI, or commitment well beyond merely doing the job; **clear, decisive leadership** in ETSI or an ETSI committee during a **critical phase** of ETSI's development, or in developing an ETSI technology; or being an **"unsung hero"** — long-term contribution, particularly at technical level, **perhaps at the expense of their corporate careers**. Each nomination is assessed on its individual merits.
  - **Award Committee (§2.4)**: composed of the **General Assembly Chair and Vice-Chairs, the Board Chair and the Director-General**; the Secretariat provides support. **Current Award Committee members are ineligible** to receive a Fellowship.
  - **Call for nomination (§2.5)**: nominations in writing, **clearly justifying** why the candidate should be granted a Fellowship, sent to the Director-General on a pre-defined form; nominations may be made **at any time**; awards are **announced at the General Assembly**.
- **Retired Friends of ETSI (§3)** — the administrative route, notably self-initiated:
  - **Eligibility**: any former ETSI Chair or Vice-Chair; any former Board member; any former member of the Secretariat.
  - **Application**: any individual in those categories **may apply**; applications in writing, clearly listing **the organization represented, the role(s) held, the duration and the technical group(s)**; sent **at any time** to the Director-General; assessed and eventually **approved by the Director-General** — no GA or Board decision is involved.
- **Rights and obligations (§4)** — the operative part:
  - **No authority to speak for ETSI**: neither a courtesy title, nor a Fellowship, nor Retired Friends membership entitles the person to give **any testimony, opinion, recommendation or advice in the name of ETSI**. If asked to do so, they **shall inform the Director-General**. **Misuse** of a courtesy title may lead the General Assembly to decide a **withdrawal** of the title.
  - **Meeting attendance (§4.1)**: honorees may be invited **at the discretion of the respective Chair or the Director-General** to attend meetings. The Guest has **no voting rights** and **shall refrain from participating in any factual and/or technical debate unless invited to do so**.
  - **Documentation access (§4.2)**: at the **Director-General's discretion** and following a **prior written request**, honorees may obtain copies of documentation. Any authorisation **must be given in writing** and is conditioned on the documentation being **freely reproduced for private and/or scientific use only** — **distribution, display, communication to the public or adaptation (including translation) is not permitted**.

## Key Concepts

- **Consensus grant**: the General Assembly's basis for courtesy titles.
- **Four-nominator rule**: the Fellowship's anti-club threshold — four individuals outside the nominee's member and Group.
- **One nomination per call**: the per-person cap on proposing Fellows.
- **Award Committee**: GA Chair + Vice-Chairs + Board Chair + Director-General; members ineligible for the award.
- **Posthumous award**: expressly permitted for Fellowships.
- **"Unsung hero"**: the named recognition category for long technical contribution at personal cost.
- **Retired Friends**: DG-approved status, self-initiated by application.
- **Private/scientific use condition**: the limit attaching to any documentation supplied to honorees.

## Mental Models

- "**Three honours, three gatekeepers**" — GA (courtesy title), Award Committee (Fellowship), DG (Retired Friends).
- "**Recognition is not a platform**" — an honour confers no right to speak for ETSI, and any request to do so goes to the Director-General.
- "**No automatic Fellowship**" — position and tenure alone earn nothing; the criteria are contribution, leadership or long unsung service.
- "**Confidential in, permission out**" — nominations stay confidential, and an awarded nominee's permission is needed before publishing details.
- "**Documents are lent, not given**" — private and scientific use only; no distribution, adaptation or translation.

## Anti-patterns

- **Expecting the Director-General to grant a courtesy title**: titles are the General Assembly's, by consensus.
- **Assuming an ETSI Fellow can speak for ETSI**: expressly denied; the person must inform the Director-General if asked.
- **Self-nominating or club-nominating for a Fellowship**: four nominators are required, from outside the nominee's member and Group — and Award Committee members cannot propose.
- **Publishing an award before consent**: the awarded person's permission is required.
- **Assuming long service guarantees a Fellowship**: there is no automatic granting; the "unsung hero" category is a criterion, not a tenure reward.
- **Treating supplied documentation as distributable**: private and scientific use only, with distribution, public communication, adaptation and translation all prohibited.
- **Letting an honoree debate technical substance uninvited**: they attend as Guests, with no vote and no participation in factual or technical debate unless invited.

## Worked Example

**A retired former Board member wants to attend a Technical Body meeting and receive working documents.** Walk §4:

1. **Invitation**: attendance is at the **discretion of the respective Chair or the Director-General** (§4.1) — there is no automatic right of attendance.
2. **Capacity**: if invited, the person attends as a **Guest**, with **no voting rights**, and must **refrain from participating in any factual and/or technical debate unless invited to do so**.
3. **Documents**: copies require a **prior written request** and the Director-General's authorisation, which is given **in writing** (§4.2).
4. **Use restriction**: the documentation may be freely reproduced **for private and/or scientific use only** — any distribution, display, communication to the public, adaptation or translation is **not permitted**.
5. **Speaking for ETSI**: if anyone asks the person for an opinion or recommendation **in the name of ETSI**, they must inform the Director-General; misuse of a courtesy title can lead the GA to withdraw it (§4).

- Takeaway: honours confer access by discretion and documentation by permission — every element is discretionary, conditional and revocable.

## Key Takeaways

1. Three honours with three different gatekeepers: GA by consensus, an Award Committee on merit, and the DG by application.
2. A Fellowship requires four external nominators, is capped at one nomination per person per call, may be posthumous, and has no automatic granting — the "unsung hero" route recognises technical contribution at personal cost.
3. The Award Committee is the GA Chair and Vice-Chairs, the Board Chair and the DG — and its members cannot be awarded.
4. No honour confers authority to speak, advise or testify in ETSI's name; misuse can cost a courtesy title.
5. Honoree attendance is discretionary and non-voting, with no participation in technical debate unless invited.
6. Documentation supplied to honorees is restricted to private and scientific use, with distribution, adaptation and translation prohibited.
