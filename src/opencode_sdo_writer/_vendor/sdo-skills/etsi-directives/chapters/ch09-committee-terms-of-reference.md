# Chapter 9: Committee Terms of Reference (FC, GoSR, OCG)

Three ToRs creating the standing coordination and advisory bodies: the **Finance Committee (FC)** (GA #85a, 4 September 2025), the **Group on Standardisation Requests (GoSR)** (GA #85a, 4 September 2025), and the **Operational Coordination Group (OCG)** (Board #144a, 15 November 2023). All three share one structural trait: they **coordinate and advise; they do not decide the matters they handle**.

## Core Idea

Each body is a forum with a narrow mandate — the FC advises on money, the GoSR shepherds EC Standardisation Requests through the pipeline, and the OCG deconflicts the technical bodies. The recurring pattern is **"facilitate, do not decide"**: the FC is expressly consultative, and the GoSR *"does not accept SReqs"* — that competence belongs to the NSBG — while the OCG *refers* unresolvable issues to the Board.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Finance Committee (FC)

- **Scope (FC §1)**: assists and advises the GA, Board and DG on budgetary and financing management; **merely consultative** unless duly committed by the GA or Board; meets **at least twice per year in ordinary session, prior to each General Assembly**; determines its own methods of procedure.
- **The GA-reporting calendar (FC §2)** — the FC's tasks are pinned to the twice-yearly GA:
  - **March/April GA**: analysis of the previous year's Financial Statements; advice on adopting **Budget Guidelines** for the following year.
  - **November/December GA**: advice on the current year's financial situation; analysis of the proposed budget for the following year; advice on handling any **excess or deficit** at year end.
  - **ETSI additional services** (e.g. Forapolis): verify that proper cost-accounting rules are applied, and that **no subsidy of additional services from member-funded activities** takes place.
  - Also: analyse and advise the GA and Board on the budget effect of strategic and budgetary proposals; bring issues to their attention whenever necessary, and **must** bring important financial and budget issues to their attention; entitled to access detailed financial and budget information as foreseen in the Financial Regulations.
- **Appointment and membership (FC §3)**: members appointed by the **General Assembly** for a **two-year mandate**; the GA endeavours to ensure **fair representation of the various member categories**; additional members may be appointed at any time but only until the end of the existing mandate period; the **Director-General and an appointed Board member hold ex-officio seats**; the **GA Chair has the right to participate**; the FC Chair may invite **Guests** for particular agenda items; the Secretariat provides a **secretary**.
- **Confidentiality (FC §4)**: access to FC meetings, documents and emails is **restricted to FC members (including ex-officio members), invited Guests and supporting Secretariat staff**; the FC **adopts a confidentiality policy** as part of its internal methods of procedure.
- **Chair limits (FC §5)**: appointed by the FC from among its members for the mandate period — up to **two consecutive terms**; beyond two only if **no other candidate indicated interest** at the GA that appointed the members; after the maximum, a new series only after a **gap of at least one term**; the limits bind **both the person and the member organization / Academic, Corporate or Public Group they represent**.

### Group on Standardisation Requests (GoSR)

- **Mission (GoSR §1)**: established by the General Assembly **under the leadership of the Director-General** to ensure efficient, transparent and coordinated handling of SReqs. It is the **central forum** where the relevant Interested Parties are informed, consulted and guided through all phases of the SReq process. Two boundaries are stated explicitly: **responsibility for developing the deliverables remains with the responsible Technical Group**, and the **GoSR "does not accept SReqs"** — it facilitates discussion and follows up, while acceptance is an NSBG decision (Ch 3, Art. 20.3.3).
- **Structure (GoSR §2)**:
  - **GoSR Secretariat** — provided by the ETSI Secretariat; handles operational, administrative and coordination tasks, including gathering inputs on draft SReqs and facilitating Interested Parties.
  - **Permanent Members** — the bodies already involved in SReq handling under the Directives: **ETSI Board, OCG, NSBG, NSOG, Annex III Organisations, ETSI Counsellors**.
  - **Ad Hoc Members** — entities not listed above, joining or invited case by case: **ETSI Full members** and **Associate members** (who may join at any time), the **relevant ETSI Technical Group(s)** (identified by the Secretariat with the OCG based on the SReq's subject matter), **CEN** and **CENELEC** (invited under the Basic Cooperation Agreement where joint activity requires coordination), and **other experts** as deemed necessary.
  - **"Interested Parties"** in the ToR means the Permanent and Ad Hoc Members collectively.
  - **GoSR Channels** — the ETSI Portal tab, document repository and main email reflector open to all Interested Parties.
- **The GoSR Secretariat's responsibilities by phase (GoSR §3.1)** — the operational spine of SReq handling:
  - **A — General coordination**: act as ETSI's single point of contact for all SReq matters; maintain the **GoSR Tracking System**, Portal pages and document repository; keep up-to-date records of all SReqs including milestones, decisions and documentation; develop and maintain guidelines, templates and communication material; ensure alignment with CEN/CENELEC processes and manage joint activities.
  - **B — Pre-consultation phase**: monitor sources (**EC Annual Union Work Programme, ICT Rolling Plan, expert groups, direct Secretariat–EC/EFTA contacts**) to anticipate potential SReqs; identify the lead TG, TO and EC/EFTA contact points early, plus other implicated TGs including CEN/CENELEC TCs; create the dedicated GoSR Channel; notify Interested Parties of possible future work and invite comments; consolidate and share feedback including EC/EFTA; record all steps.
  - **C — Draft SReq phase**: acknowledge receipt; identify/confirm the lead TG, TO and EC/EFTA contacts; notify Interested Parties; create or update the Channel; **schedule and chair consultation calls/meetings to complete the "Checklist for SReq"**; facilitate consensus-building by supporting transparent discussion and mediating differing views; share the completed Checklist including EC/EFTA; manage further exchange with the European Commission; record all steps.
  - **D — Final SReq phase**: acknowledge receipt; notify Interested Parties; create/update the Channel; **create and launch a 25 calendar-day Weighted National Vote during which NSBG members are invited to vote**; coordinate a parallel examination call/meeting and **assist the NSBG Chair in chairing it**; draft a summary that **accurately reflects the tenor of the discussions, including diverging views where consensus was not reached**; after the vote, communicate results to the Interested Parties and the Director-General, coordinate the drafting of the letter by which the **Director-General alone** informs the EC/EFTA of ETSI's accept/reject decision, and coordinate a **Collective Letter** informing all ETSI members; record all steps.
  - **E — Ongoing monitoring and reporting**: verify and coordinate ETSI's compliance with **specific conditions** in each accepted SReq; track delivery of all requested deliverables to EC/EFTA; coordinate periodic and final reports; provide periodic or ad-hoc reports to the Board, GA, relevant Technical Groups and the GoSR if requested.
  - **F — Case-specific**: help disseminate deliverables and activities related to accepted SReqs; propose process adjustments for SReqs under a different setup (submitted to the relevant decision-makers); perform other tasks supporting the mission.
- **Interested Parties' obligations (GoSR §3.2)**: participate actively in examination calls and discussions; provide **timely inputs** for the Checklist for SReq; review and comment on draft and final SReqs when invited; **respect EC/EFTA and ETSI timelines**; contribute constructively to consensus-building.
- **Sub-groups (GoSR §4)**: may be established in the GoSR or any Channel, coordinated between the relevant TG Chair(s) and the GoSR Secretariat, to manage multiple or complex SReqs in parallel; led/coordinated by the GoSR Secretariat; activities **must not overlap with, nor substitute,** the responsible TG's work; **disbanded once the deliverables are complete**.
- **Communication, transparency and review (GoSR §§5–6)**: external outreach during pre-consultation to inform industry stakeholders that ETSI may be the appropriate venue; timely updates, invitations and results to all Interested Parties; strive for consistency in terminology and approach with CEN/CENELEC; the ToR and processes are reviewed as needed, with **approval of the ToR falling under the General Assembly**.

### Operational Coordination Group (OCG)

- **Scope (OCG §A)**: acts as **focal point and forum for coordination** of the Technical Bodies (Technical Committees, ETSI Projects), ISGs, SDGs and Coordination Groups; resolves **as far as possible any duplication of effort or conflict of technical views** between TBs to reinforce cooperation across the Technical Organization, ISGs, SDGs and CGs.
  - **Two identity limits**: the OCG **reports to the Board** and is **not itself a Coordination Group** as defined in RoP §5.2; it **refers to the Board issues it cannot resolve**.
- **The OCG's tasks (§A.3)**:
  - provide an opportunity for TB, ISG, SDG and CG Chairs — and ETSI representatives to externally initiated CGs — to **exchange information and experiences** and discuss issues before formal submission to the Board;
  - respond to Board requests for information; take advice from the Board on implementing ETSI policies and Board/GA decisions;
  - **make proposals to the Board for changes to the Technical Working Procedures**;
  - seek to improve effectiveness within the Technical Organization and continually monitor potential synergies among TBs, ISGs, SDGs and CGs and with external bodies;
  - **make recommendations to the Board on the allocation of resources for STFs and on the proposed technical testing roadmap and priorities for TTFs**;
  - raise major issues of concern to the Board; seek information from TBs and ISGs on new Work Items planned or commenced.
  - The OCG may create **topic-specific sub-groups and ad-hoc groups** (§A.4).
- **Meetings (§B)**: open to TB Chairs (who **shall endeavour to ensure their TB is properly represented**), ISG Chairs, SDG Chairs, Chairs of ETSI-initiated CGs, ETSI representatives to external CGs, Board members, the Director-General, Secretariat members, Counsellors, and **ETSI members as Guests**. The OCG meets as required; specific-issue meetings may be convened at the OCG Chair's request, and **requests by three (3) or more TB Chairs require the OCG to call an additional meeting** — a rare bottom-up trigger in ETSI's machinery. The agenda is structured by subject or work. The OCG works **on a Consensus basis wherever possible**, though **indicative voting by a nominated representative from each TB may be used for Consensus building**. It is **chaired by a Vice-Chair of the Board** (or another Board member if the Vice-Chair is unavailable).
- **Sub-groups and ad-hoc groups (§C)**: ad-hoc groups for specific issues at the OCG's request; sub-groups to cover OCG matters targeting a particular set of topics involving all or a defined subset of TBs; **the OCG establishes their Terms of Reference and appoints their Chairs**.

## Key Concepts

- **Consultative by default (FC)**: advisory unless the GA or Board commits the FC to more.
- **25-day Weighted National Vote**: the SReq acceptance vote the GoSR launches for NSBG members.
- **"Checklist for SReq"**: the structured instrument completed with Interested Parties in the draft-SReq phase.
- **Collective Letter**: the DG's communication to all ETSI members about an accept/reject decision.
- **GoSR Tracking System**: the record of every SReq step, milestone and decision.
- **"Does not accept SReqs"**: the GoSR's explicit competence boundary; acceptance is the NSBG's.
- **Indicative voting for consensus building (OCG)**: a non-decisional straw poll.
- **Three-TB-Chair trigger**: the mechanism forcing an OCG meeting.
- **OCG ≠ Coordination Group**: the definitional clarification in RoP §5.2.

## Mental Models

- "**Forums, not deciders**" — every one of these bodies facilitates or advises; none decides the substance it handles.
- "**FC runs on the GA calendar**" — its reporting obligations are keyed to the March/April and November/December GA meetings.
- "**The GoSR is the SReq air-traffic controller**" — it tracks, convenes, checks and reports, while the NSBG votes and the TG delivers.
- "**One voice out: the Director-General**" — the GoSR coordinates the drafting, but only the DG sends the letter to the EC/EFTA.
- "**The OCG is the technical body's common room, chaired by the Board**" — coordination across TBs, with a Board Vice-Chair in the chair and escalation upward.

## Anti-patterns

- **Expecting the GoSR to accept a Standardisation Request**: expressly outside its role — the NSBG decides acceptance.
- **Expecting the Finance Committee to decide financial matters**: merely consultative unless duly committed by the GA or Board.
- **Letting GoSR sub-groups take over the Technical Group's work**: they must neither overlap with nor substitute the TG's responsibilities.
- **Summarising an SReq examination call without the dissent**: the GoSR must record diverging views where consensus was not reached.
- **Treating the OCG as a Coordination Group under RoP §5.2**: the ToR states the contrary.
- **Ignoring the FC's access restriction**: only members (including ex-officio), invited Guests and supporting Secretariat staff may access its meetings, documents and emails.
- **Assuming a third of TB Chairs can only ask politely**: three or more TB Chairs *require* an additional OCG meeting.

## Worked Example

**An EC Standardisation Request arrives.** Trace the division of labour:

1. **Anticipation**: the GoSR Secretariat has already monitored the EC's Annual Union Work Programme and Rolling Plan, and notified Interested Parties of possible future work (GoSR §3.1.B).
2. **Draft phase**: the Secretariat schedules and chairs consultation calls to complete the **"Checklist for SReq"**, mediating differing views (GoSR §3.1.C).
3. **Final phase**: the Secretariat **launches a 25 calendar-day Weighted National Vote** for NSBG members and runs a parallel examination call — **assisting the NSBG Chair** in chairing it (GoSR §3.1.D). The examination runs in parallel with the NSB vote (RoP Art. 20.3.1).
4. **Decision**: **the NSBG decides** acceptance/rejection — the GoSR does not accept SReqs. The DG must notify the EC, EFTA and Interested Parties **within 30 calendar days** of receipt (RoP Art. 20.3.3).
5. **Communication**: the GoSR coordinates the drafting of the EC/EFTA letter, but **only the Director-General sends it**; a Collective Letter informs all ETSI members.
6. **Delivery**: responsibility for developing the deliverables stays with the **responsible Technical Group** (GoSR §1); the GoSR tracks delivery, compliance with SReq conditions, and periodic and final reports (GoSR §3.1.E).
7. **Coordination**: if the work touches another TG or CEN/CENELEC, the OCG is the forum for deconflicting effort (OCG §A), referring anything unresolvable to the Board.

- Takeaway: three bodies, three distinct roles — and the one thing none of them does is make the acceptance decision.

## Key Takeaways

1. The FC advises on money on a GA-keyed calendar, is consultative by default, meets at least twice a year, and controls access to its own materials under a self-adopted confidentiality policy.
2. The GoSR is the SReq coordination and tracking machinery across pre-consultation, draft, final, monitoring and case-specific phases — but it does not accept SReqs.
3. The SReq acceptance vote is a **25 calendar-day Weighted National Vote** of NSBG members; the DG notifies the EC/EFTA within 30 calendar days.
4. Only the Director-General communicates ETSI's decision externally; TG responsibility for deliverables is untouched by GoSR coordination.
5. The OCG is the cross-TB deconfliction forum, chaired by a Board Vice-Chair, working by consensus with indicative voting for consensus building, and may be forced to meet by three TB Chairs.
6. All three bodies facilitate rather than decide — unresolved matters go to the Board or the GA.
