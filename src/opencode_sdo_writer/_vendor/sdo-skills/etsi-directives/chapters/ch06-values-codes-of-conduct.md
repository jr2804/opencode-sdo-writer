# Chapter 6: ETSI Values and Codes of Conduct

Three General-Assembly instruments: **ETSI Values** (GA #81a, 4 May 2023), the **Code of Conduct for ETSI Members** (GA #87a, 27 August 2026), and the **Code of Conduct for Board Members** (GA #87a, 27 August 2026). Together they set the behavioural layer that the procedural instruments assume.

## Core Idea

The Values state *what ETSI stands for* (European and global democratic principles, human rights, and the seven international-standardization principles); the two Codes say *how individuals must behave* to live up to them — with the Members' Code supplying the operational hook that Chairs must **remind delegates of the Code, the antitrust rules and the IPR rules at the start of every meeting**, and the Board's Code binding Board members to loyalty, care, confidentiality and conflict-of-interest discipline, with **annual written certification**.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **ETSI Values — the legal-value baseline**: ETSI, located in France and the EU, follows French and EU law and values:
  - the **Treaty on European Union** (as amended by the Lisbon Treaty);
  - the **Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union**;
  - the **European Declaration on Digital Rights and Principles for the Digital Decade**;
  - a **secular** operating environment;
  - the **European Convention on Human Rights** and other Council of Europe agreements;
  - globally, the **UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights** as a reference point, and the **UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities**;
  - and compliance with relevant EU regulations, including **Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012** on European Standardisation (as amended by 2022/2480).
  - These documents "constitute ETSI's understanding of European values".
- **The seven standardization principles (Values §"Technical standardisation")**: ETSI respects the WTO/TBT Agreement's **Code of Good Practice for the Preparation, Adoption and Application of Standards** and its **Principles for the Development of International Standards**, namely **coherence, transparency, openness, consensus, voluntary application, independence from special interests, and efficiency** — which are also the founding principles of the European Standardisation System.
  - Operational consequences stated in the Values: direct participation of all interested parties; an open environment promoting inclusiveness; **ETSI is contribution driven**; deliverables support global trade and connectivity and promote **fair competition**; they respond to market needs and, where appropriate, to regulatory or societal needs; and **deliverables are accessible to everyone free of charge and without the need to provide personal data**.
- **Diversity and respect**: value every human being, treat everyone with respect; act against discrimination on grounds including gender, race, colour, ethnic or social origin, genetic features, language, religion or belief, political or any other opinion, membership of a national minority, property, birth, disability, age and sexual orientation; encourage freedom of speech and expression through respectful open exchange.
- **Environment**: ETSI deliverables contribute to combating climate change, increasing sustainability and achieving climate neutrality; environmental protection is considered in actions, aiming to improve the ecological footprint.
- **Promotion**: ETSI broadly informs about its Values, creating awareness in all groups, technical bodies and committees.
- **Members' Code — nature and status**: a broad guide to appropriate behaviour "particularly in cases where specific rules are not available"; it **augments the Directives but does not override them**; it complements more specific codes such as the Board's Code.
- **Members' Code — general acknowledgements**: delegates acknowledge that ETSI was set up by **CEPT** (European administrations, industry partners and stakeholder groups) and is recognized as such; they should support ETSI operations including the relationship with European administrations, noting the needs of the **EU and EFTA** and the advice provided by Counsellors; and they acknowledge the **principles of international standardization** — consensus, transparency, openness, impartiality, effectiveness, relevance and coherence — that **respect for other delegates, the Secretariat and the professional culture of standardization is foremost**, and that **consensus-building should be upheld**.
- **Members' Code — the conduct rules "when involved in ETSI activities"**:
  - act in meetings and discussions **in accordance with ETSI Values**;
  - keep discussions moderate, professional, respectful and friendly, without prejudice;
  - unless acting in official roles, delegates are **assumed to present ideas according to their best professional judgement**;
  - act in good faith with due care and diligence; **avoid collusive, anticompetitive or dominant behaviour**; promote a fair and ethical culture;
  - act on a fully informed basis, with due diligence, to engage constructively;
  - actively participate, providing **timely contributions uploaded to the ETSI portal**;
  - value diversity and act against discrimination as set out in the Values;
  - **speakers shall not be interrupted**; delegates speak once **recognized by the convenor/Chair**; interventions should be short;
  - take into account the views of **all** attendees, **including those whose first language is not English**;
  - **inform the Chair or the Secretariat of any issue requiring escalation** so it can be solved in a timely manner, and use the appropriate resolution mechanisms, respecting their outcomes;
  - **avoid conflicts of interest** and **immediately disclose** any actual or potential conflict through the appropriate mechanisms;
  - take into account the interests and objectives of the **European Union** (legislation, policy documents, or as outlined by ETSI's Counsellors) when developing deliverables.
- **Reporting and investigation of misconduct**: ETSI takes any allegation seriously; **any person** involved in ETSI activities — delegates, elected officials, Secretariat staff, other participants — may report observed or suspected misconduct; incident reports are treated **confidentially** and must contain **sufficient information to enable an appropriate investigation**; all parties involved, including the reporting person and the person concerned, are heard. Reports include in particular **partiality or unwillingness by Chairs or Vice-Chairs to seek consensus**.
- **Members' Code — the additional duties of Chairs and Vice-Chairs** (the operational core):
  - act in their official roles according to **best professional and neutral judgement, independent of the interests of their supporting organization**;
  - facilitate discussions **across cultures, inclusively**, so that decisions align with ETSI Values, protection of **minority rights**, gender neutrality;
  - **maintain strict impartiality** and act in the interest of ETSI and its members;
  - **ensure the ETSI Guidelines for Antitrust Compliance are followed**;
  - **remind delegates of the highlights of the Code at the start of each meeting — at the same time as the Antitrust and IPR reminders**;
  - report, as far as necessary, **discriminatory, disrespectful, unprofessional or prejudiced behaviour**.
- **Board Code — general statement of expectation**: each Board member adheres to a high standard of ethical conduct and acts in accordance with ETSI's Vision, Mission, Values and commitments; the good name of ETSI depends on how Board members conduct business **and how the public perceives that conduct**; unethical, misleading or ambiguous actions — **or any conduct that might lead to such an interpretation** — cannot be accepted; these principles do not limit the duties, obligations or legal requirements Board members must comply with.
- **Board Code — the five principles**:
  - **Loyalty**: not be, or appear to be, subject to influences, interests or relationships conflicting with ETSI's interests or its ability to operate for the benefit of the community; serve ETSI's organization and global community and protect its interests, staff, assets and legal rights; when acting in support of EU policies and legislation, take the EU's interests and objectives into account.
  - **Care**: act with seriousness and diligence in Board and committee affairs; act prudently in oversight of ETSI's technical activities; be **familiar with the Directives** and the environment ETSI operates in and understand its activity, policies, strategies, Vision and Mission; take the steps necessary to be **sufficiently informed** to decide and participate; **attend all Board meetings** except where exceptional circumstances make attendance impractical.
  - **Compliance with laws, rules and regulations** applicable to ETSI — with the **Legal and Governance department** available for advice.
  - **Observance of ethical standards**: honesty, fairness, integrity at the highest level.
  - **Avoiding conflicts of interest**: perform the role with utmost integrity and prudence, act in the interests of the Institute and the membership, avoid conflicts; use best professional judgement **including avoiding improper influence when offered opportunities, gifts or entertainment**; **declare any conflict** — for example where ETSI's interests conflict with one's own or with those of one's employer or supporting ETSI member; note that because ETSI is governed by French law, conflict of interest is also subject to French rules; the Legal and Governance Director assists in doubt.
- **Board Code — confidentiality and media**:
  - maintain the confidentiality of information entrusted to them **when clearly indicated as confidential**, except where disclosure is authorized or legally required;
  - on media interaction: the Board ensures no ETSI communication breaches confidentiality and related principles; **the legal responsibility for ETSI falls on the Director-General, who is the one entitled to speak for the Institute** — any other public intervention must respect that allocation.
- **Board Code — acting in personal capacity**: Board members act in an **individual capacity** and represent the **common interests of the ETSI membership**; they must make clear that they represent ETSI and avoid any possible confusion.
- **Board Code — enforcement, affirmation, review**:
  - breaches, **intentional or not**, are reported to the **Director-General in accordance with Article 22 of the Rules of Procedure**;
  - following a DG investigation, a report goes to the Board **excluding any Board members concerned**; where appropriate further steps follow;
  - breaches **may constitute grounds for initiating dismissal procedures** against the member concerned, under ETSI rules and/or applicable law;
  - **all Board members shall read the Code at least once per year and certify in writing** that they have done so and understand and are willing to adhere to it;
  - the Code is **reviewed periodically** by the Legal and Governance department, which recommends changes or rescission to the full Board.

## Key Concepts

- **The seven standardization principles**: coherence, transparency, openness, consensus, voluntary application, independence from special interests, efficiency.
- **Contribution driven**: ETSI's deliverables derive from member contributions — hence the duty to upload timely contributions to the portal.
- **Counsellors**: EC/EFTA representatives whose advice delegates must take into account.
- **Recognized by CEPT**: the founding acknowledgement behind the Members' Code.
- **Misconduct report**: confidential, must contain enough information to investigate; both reporter and the person concerned are heard.
- **Chair impartiality**: official-role judgement independent of the supporting organization.
- **The three reminders**: Code + Antitrust + IPR at the start of every meeting.
- **Annual certification**: the Board Code's written affirmation obligation.
- **Vision, Mission, Values**: the reference frame for Board conduct.

## Mental Models

- "**Values are the constitution, Codes are the conduct rules**" — one states identity, the others regulate behaviour.
- "**The Chair sets the tone in the first five minutes**" — the Code/Antitrust/IPR reminder trio is a required ritual, and impartiality is the Chair's defining duty.
- "**Appearance is standard for Board conduct**" — conduct that *might lead to* a misleading interpretation cannot be accepted.
- "**Perception is part of the duty**" — the good name of ETSI depends on how conduct is perceived, not only on what was done.
- "**Speak once, when recognized, and let others be heard**" — the meeting-etiquette rules are part of the Code, not mere chairing preference.
- "**One voice for ETSI: the Director-General**" — media and public statements are allocated, not open.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating the Code as overriding the Directives**: the Members' Code expressly augments but does not override them; where a specific rule exists, it governs.
- **Skipping the opening reminders**: Chairs "shall" remind delegates of the Code highlights together with the Antitrust and IPR reminders at the start of each meeting.
- **Chairing in the interest of one's supporting organization**: the Code requires strict impartiality and independence from the supporting organization's interests.
- **Leaving a conflict of interest undeclared**: both Codes require declaration — the Members' Code immediately, the Board Code with French-law conflict rules attached.
- **Board members speaking publicly for ETSI**: only the Director-General is entitled to speak for the Institute.
- **Failing to certify**: Board members must read the Code yearly and certify in writing.
- **Ignoring minority rights in the drive to consensus**: the Code names protection of minority rights as part of inclusive chairing, and partiality or unwillingness to seek consensus as reportable misconduct.
- **Overlooking non-native English speakers**: the Code requires their views to be taken into consideration.

## Worked Example

**Opening a Technical Body meeting.** The Chair's first minutes are prescribed; walk the sequence:

1. **Antitrust reminder** — per the Guidelines for Antitrust Compliance and the Members' Code, and specifically the topic prohibitions (Ch 5, C.4.1).
2. **IPR call** — the mandatory "Call for IPRs" per the IPR Guide §2.3.2/Annex C, with the note that declarations go through ipr.etsi.org (Ch 4).
3. **Code of Conduct highlights** — the Members' Code requires the Chair to remind delegates of the CoC highlights **at the same time** as the antitrust and IPR reminders.
4. **Meeting conduct** — speakers are recognized by the Chair and not interrupted; interventions are short; views of non-native English speakers are explicitly to be taken into account; the Chair keeps discussions moderate, respectful and free of prejudice.
5. **Escalation** — any delegate with an issue requiring escalation informs the Chair or the Secretariat so it can be resolved in time; the member uses the appropriate mechanisms and respects their outcomes.
6. **Impartiality** — throughout, the Chair acts on professional and neutral judgement, independent of the interests of the supporting organization, and protects minority rights in consensus-building.

- Takeaway: the opening ritual bundles three separate normative duties into one moment — and failure to perform it is itself a breach, because C.3/C.4 of the antitrust Guidelines and the Members' Code each make it mandatory.

## Key Takeaways

1. The Values anchor ETSI in a listed set of EU/global human-rights and standardization instruments, and in the seven standardization principles — with deliverables free of charge and without personal data.
2. The Members' Code augments the Directives but does not override them; it sets meeting conduct, contribution duties, escalation, conflict disclosure and the EU-interest consideration.
3. Chairs carry extra duties: impartiality independent of their supporting organization, inclusive chairing, antitrust compliance, the opening Code/Antitrust/IPR reminder, and reporting misconduct.
4. Misconduct reporting is open to everyone involved in ETSI activities, is confidential, and must carry enough information to investigate.
5. The Board's Code binds to loyalty, care, legal compliance, ethical standards and conflict-of-interest discipline — judged partly on *appearance* — plus confidentiality, media discipline and annual written certification.
6. Breaches are routed to the Director-General under RoP Article 22 and can ground dismissal proceedings.
