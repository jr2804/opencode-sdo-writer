# Chapter 4: ETSI IPR Policy and the Guide on IPR

Two instruments, one regime: the **ETSI IPR Policy** is *Annex 6 to the Rules of Procedure* (normative, version carried by the RoP of 27 August 2026), while the **Guide on Intellectual Property Rights** (Board #133, 10 June 2021) is the explanatory companion. The Guide states plainly: *"this guide ... does not replace the ETSI IPR Policy which takes precedence in all cases."*

## Core Idea

The regime is **FRAND, not royalty-free**: members keep their IPRs (including the right to refuse licensing), but when an IPR is ESSENTIAL to a standard the Director-General requests an undertaking to license on fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms — and the machinery's real purpose is to surface Essential IPRs **early enough that ETSI is not wasting effort on a deliverable that a late disclosure could block**.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Policy objectives (Clause 3)**: create STANDARDS and TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS based on solutions best meeting the technical objectives; reward IPR holders (members, their AFFILIATES, and third parties) adequately and fairly; take reasonable measures so that preparation, adoption and application of standards enable wide use.
- **Disclosure (Clause 4)** — the load-bearing obligation:
  - **4.1**: each MEMBER shall use **reasonable endeavours**, in particular during development of a STANDARD or TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION **where it participates**, to inform ETSI of ESSENTIAL IPRs it becomes aware of.
  - **4.2**: **no obligation to conduct IPR searches.**
  - **4.3**: the 4.1 obligations are deemed fulfilled for all existing and future members of a **PATENT FAMILY** once ETSI has been informed of one member of that family.
  - *In the Guide's terms*: a "reasonable endeavours" duty, deliberately not an obligation to search — a distinction worth restating whenever someone asserts ETSI imposes a search duty.
- **Availability of licences (Clause 6)** — the FRAND engine:
  - **6.1**: when an ESSENTIAL IPR is brought to ETSI's attention, the **Director-General immediately requests the owner to give an undertaking** to grant licences on fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms. Scope of the undertaking: **MANUFACTURE** (including the right to make or have made customized components and subsystems to the licensee's own design for use in MANUFACTURE); **sell, lease or otherwise dispose of EQUIPMENT so manufactured**; **repair, use or operate EQUIPMENT**; and **use METHODS**. It may be **conditioned on reciprocity**.
  - **6.1bis**: FRAND undertakings are interpreted as **encumbrances binding all successors-in-interest** — the anti-"patent transfer evasion" clause.
  - **6.2**: an undertaking for one member of a PATENT FAMILY applies to **all existing and future ESSENTIAL IPRs of that family**, absent an explicit statement to the contrary.
  - **6.3**: while the undertaking is not granted, **COMMITTEE Chairs** should, if appropriate and in consultation with the Secretariat, use judgment as to whether to continue development.
  - **6.4**: at the request of the EC and/or EFTA, ETSI shall arrange cost studies for a specific standard or class of standards.
- **Mandatory forms (Clause 6bis)**: MEMBERS **shall** use one of the ETSI IPR Licensing Declaration forms in the Appendix. Two forms exist:
  - the **IPR Information Statement and Licensing Declaration** — submitted through the ETSI IPR online database (ipr.etsi.org) with its Annex; and
  - the **General IPR Licensing Declaration** — an undertaking covering any IPR that is or becomes essential to the identified standards; using it does **not** remove the duty to declare essential patents.
  - Paper declarations only exceptionally, when instructed by the Secretariat, returned to the ETSI Legal Department.
- **Information by ETSI (Clause 7)**: every published STANDARD or TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION must include information on ESSENTIAL IPRs brought to ETSI's attention before publication, and ETSI must maintain access procedures. Operationally this is **ETSI SR 000 314** plus the **IPR Online Database** — and on conflict, **the database takes precedence**.
- **Non-availability of licences (Clause 8)** — the escalation ladder when FRAND fails:
  - **Before publication (8.1)**: if the owner refuses, the **General Assembly** reviews the requirement and satisfies itself that a viable alternative exists, is **not blocked by that IPR**, and meets ETSI's requirements (8.1.1). If no viable alternative exists, **work on the standard shall cease** (8.1.2), and the DG follows distinct paths — for a **MEMBER** owner: request reconsideration, then obtain a written explanation, then send it with GA minute extracts to the **Counsellors**; for a **third party**: request supporting details from the complainant, write to the owner requesting licences, and if the owner refuses or **does not answer within three months**, escalate. Chairs may use judgment (with the Secretariat) whether to pursue development (8.1.3).
  - **After publication (8.2)**: the DG gathers supporting details, writes to the owner (three-month answer window), informs the GA (which votes), consults the Counsellors if the vote fails, and finally may **request the European Commission to consider further action, including non-recognition of the standard**. Throughout, due account is taken of the interests of enterprises that have already invested in implementing it.
- **ETSI ownership of IPRs (Clause 9)**: copyright in standards/technical-specification documentation and reports **vests in ETSI** (9.1), with due acknowledgement given. For **SOFTWARE** (9.2): there is no requirement to use the software; a contributing MEMBER grants ETSI, members and implementers a licence **without monetary compensation** for evaluation, publication, and inclusion in compliant implementations (9.2.1); the copyright licence extends to implementers on FRAND terms (9.2.2); contributions come "**AS IS**" with the contributor warranting it has the necessary rights (9.2.3); and **no patent licence is granted by implication or estoppel** (9.2.4). ETSI seeks ownership of other IPRs only for employee- or contractor-generated IPRs (9.3), and grants non-members licences on fair and reasonable terms for IPRs it owns outside 9.1 (9.4).
- **Confidentiality (Clause 10)**: COMMITTEE proceedings are **non-confidential by default**. Information is confidential only if **all three** criteria hold: in written or tangible form; **identified in writing as confidential when submitted**; and first submitted to and **accepted by the Chair as confidential**. Confidential information incorporated in a standard **becomes non-confidential from publication**.
- **Reproduction (Clause 11)**: MEMBERS may copy standards documentation **for their own use free of charge but may not distribute**.
- **Law (Clause 12)**: French law governs; no MEMBER is obliged to breach its own national or supranational law.
- **Policy decisions (Clause 13)**: no decision relating to implementation of the POLICY may be taken unless supported by a **71 % majority of the weighted individual votes**.
- **Violation (Clause 14)**: a violation by a MEMBER is a **breach of its obligations to ETSI**, with the General Assembly deciding the action to be taken.
- **The ESSENTIAL test (Clause 15.6)**: it is not possible **on technical (but not commercial) grounds**, taking into account normal technical practice and the state of the art generally available at the time of standardization, to make, sell, lease, otherwise dispose of, repair, use or operate EQUIPMENT or METHODS complying with a standard **without infringing the IPR**. Put plainly: if you cannot implement the standard without using the IPR, it is Essential — and *commercial* considerations are irrelevant.
- **Other Clause 15 definitions worth holding**: **PATENT FAMILY** = all documents sharing at least one priority, including the priority document(s); **AFFILIATE** = entities linked by >50 % ownership or control (and references to a MEMBER include its AFFILIATES); **COMMITTEE** = any Technical Body, including ETSI Projects, Technical Committees, ETSI Partnership Projects and their Working Groups; **IPR** = statute-law rights including applications but **excluding trademarks, trade secrets, confidential information and get-up**; **STANDARD** and **TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION** are adopted on the date the technical content became available to all MEMBERS; **SOFTWARE** expressly includes ASN.1/TTCN/XML data and stream structure definitions and schema examples such as SDL diagrams and data flowcharts.
- **The Chairs' operational duties (Guide §2.3 + normative Annex C)** — the most actionable part of the regime:
  - **Scope statements must be properly defined** for every Work Item, containing: a broad statement of the technical field; description of broad system concepts; identification of any standard the WI is likely to be based on; a list of features the standard will define or limit; a technical description of each feature in broad terms; and a list of criteria the standard must satisfy.
  - **A "Call for IPRs" at the beginning of every Technical Body and working group meeting** — oral or written, using the supplied example text; reinforced by **additional reminders at defined milestones**: formal submission of a technical solution; completion of a first stable draft; working-group approval of a draft standard; TB approval of a draft standard.
  - **Record that the call was performed** and **record responses (or their absence)**; inform the Secretariat.
  - **Immediately inform the ETSI Legal Department** on becoming aware of an Essential or potentially Essential IPR.
  - **Record any copyright identified** (or its absence) and inform the Secretariat.
- **Duties members do NOT have (Guide §2.2)**: no duty to conduct IPR searches; **no duty to disclose their commercial licensing terms inside the Technical Body** — those are commercial matters between companies and are not addressed within ETSI (Guide §4.1). Voluntary, unilateral, public **ex ante** disclosure of licensing terms is possible, and ETSI may act as a depository, but **no member is obliged to disclose terms**.
- **Late disclosure and "Intentional Delay" (Guide §2)**: the harms from late disclosure are licences that are unavailable at all, or available but not on FRAND terms — the remedy being changing the standard, in extremes restarting it. **"Timeliness" cannot be defined** (a definition would itself be a change to the Policy). "Intentional Delay" is a *description* — deliberately withholding disclosures significantly beyond what normal "Timeliness" considerations would expect — and where **proven**, it is a breach of the Policy (Clause 14) **sanctionable by the General Assembly**.
- **ETSI does not verify (Guide §3.2)**: as a general principle ETSI performs **no check on the status or validity** of notified Essential IPRs and **no search** for un-notified ones; the database is raw received data, so potential licensees should use it at their discretion and contact the patent holder.
- **Transfer of ownership and disputes (Guide §4.2–4.4)**: Clause 6.1bis applies to transfers; ETSI encourages **non-members, including courts**, to read FRAND undertakings as encumbrances, and encourages prospective transferees to search the IPR database. Disputes should first be resolved bilaterally and amicably, then the members are invited to inform the GA (mediation by other members and/or the Secretariat) — but **once a patent is granted, absent agreement, national courts alone have authority**. NDAs may be used to protect commercial interests during licensing negotiations.

## Key Concepts

- **FRAND**: fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory licensing — the regime's default, with no preference for royalty-free licensing.
- **ESSENTIAL**: impossible on technical (not commercial) grounds to implement without infringement.
- **PATENT FAMILY**: documents sharing at least one priority.
- **AFFILIATE**: >50 % ownership/control grouping; MEMBER references include AFFILIATES.
- **Successors-in-interest encumbrance**: FRAND undertakings follow transferred IPRs (6.1bis).
- **Reciprocity condition**: the licence undertaking may be conditioned on the licensee reciprocating.
- **SR 000 314 / IPR Online Database**: the two public information channels; database prevails on conflict.
- **Intentional Delay**: proven deliberate withholding beyond normal timeliness — a sanctionable breach.
- **AS IS software**: contributed software carries no implied warranties and no implied patent licence.

## Mental Models

- "**FRAND by default, royalty-free is not the point**" — the regime balances access against reward; a GA decision even reaffirmed that the software-copyright changes are not a shift toward royalty-free preference.
- "**Declare, don't search**" — the obligation is reasonable endeavours to inform, explicitly not a search duty.
- "**Early knowledge prevents wasted work**" — the entire regime exists so a blocking IPR surfaces before the standard is built around it.
- "**The Chair is the sensor**" — scope statements + the Call for IPRs + milestone reminders are how the Institute learns of Essential IPRs in time.
- "**The database is a registry, not an audit**" — ETSI records what it is told and verifies nothing; diligence is the licensee's.
- "**Technical, not commercial**" — essentiality is a technical test; licensing terms stay outside ETSI.

## Anti-patterns

- **Asserting a duty to search for patents**: expressly disclaimed by Clause 4.2 and Guide §2.2.
- **Discussing licensing terms or royalty rates in a Technical Body**: the Guide states TBs are not the appropriate place — licensing terms are commercial matters outside ETSI, and doing so also engages the antitrust guidelines (Ch 5).
- **Treating the Guide as authoritative over the Policy**: the Guide defers to the Policy in all cases.
- **Skipping the Call for IPRs or failing to record it**: the call is mandatory at the start of every TB/WG meeting, with milestone reminders and a recording duty — Annex C is *normative*.
- **Assuming confidential information stays confidential because it was marked in a slide**: all three Clause 10 criteria must hold, including the Chair's acceptance.
- **Believing a declared patent is validated by ETSI**: no validity, relevance or essentiality check is performed.
- **Ignoring the three-month window in Clause 8**: an IPR owner's silence for three months is itself an escalation trigger.
- **Expecting the FRAND undertaking to lapse on a change of ownership**: 6.1bis binds successors-in-interest.

## Worked Example

**A member discovers, in a TB meeting, that a competitor's patent may be essential.** Walk the regime:

1. **Scope**: check the Work Item's scope statement is properly defined (Guide §2.3.1) — a vague scope defeats the whole disclosure mechanism.
2. **Disclosure**: the member uses reasonable endeavours to inform ETSI (Policy 4.1); it need not search (4.2), and informing ETSI about one member of a PATENT FAMILY covers the family (4.3).
3. **Form**: the declaration goes through the IPR Online Database using the Information Statement and Licensing Declaration form (6bis, Guide §2.1.3).
4. **Chair's action**: the Chair informs the ETSI Legal Department **immediately** on becoming aware of an Essential or potentially Essential IPR, and records the Call for IPRs and the response in the minutes (Guide §2.3.4, Annex C).
5. **DG's action**: the Director-General **immediately requests** the FRAND undertaking (6.1); the undertaking, once given, covers the whole patent family (6.2) and binds successors-in-interest (6.1bis).
6. **If the owner refuses** — and the standard is not yet published — the GA satisfies itself a viable, unblocked alternative exists (8.1.1); with no alternative, **work ceases** (8.1.2) and the DG's member-owner path (reconsideration → written explanation → Counsellors) runs, with the three-month answer window if the owner stays silent.
7. **If licences disappear after publication** — the 8.2 ladder culminates in a request to the European Commission, including possible **non-recognition of the standard**.

- Takeaway: the regime is a chain of named duties and clocks, not a single rule — and the only party who can start the chain in time is the Chair at the meeting.

## Key Takeaways

1. The IPR Policy is normative Annex 6 to the RoP; the Guide explains it and yields to it on conflict.
2. The regime is FRAND with no royalty-free preference; members keep their IPRs and may refuse to license.
3. Disclosure is "reasonable endeavours" where you participate — explicitly **no duty to search**, and one family member's disclosure covers the family.
4. The DG immediately requests the FRAND undertaking; the undertaking covers the patent family and binds successors-in-interest.
5. Essentiality is a **technical, not commercial** test; licensing terms and rates stay out of ETSI's Technical Bodies.
6. The Chairs' duties (scope statements, mandatory Call for IPRs, milestone reminders, recording, immediate reporting) are normative — read Annex C as a checklist.
7. Late or intentionally delayed disclosure can be sanctioned by the General Assembly as a breach of the Policy.
